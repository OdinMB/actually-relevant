# Story research (admin action and pipeline step)

An editor can research one story from its edit form, and the research step (the `research_stories` job, "The pipeline step" below) researches the selected stories, in apply mode before they publish: a run searches the news with Tavily, extracts facts from up to six sources (the original article included) and from our own earlier coverage, writes a cited draft, checks it, and records a verdict. **A run writes only its own `story_research_runs` row: it never moves a status, never writes a story field and never applies its verdict.** None of the public routes, RSS, the newsletter, the podcast or social posts reads the table. **A run reaches readers only when its version is applied**: by an editor ("Applying a run" below), or by the research step in apply mode, which also rejects a story research found to be old news or contradicted. The apply writes the researched texts and research's emotion tag into the story, which keeps its own rating, and from then on every reader of the story's fields takes them. Design: the multi-source stories design of 2026-11-12; plans `.plans/2026-11-12_story-research-admin.md`, `.plans/2026-11-12_researched-stories-reader.md` (steps 1 to 3 built) and `.plans/completed/2026-11-13_research-own-words.md` ("Own words" below). Why runs have their own table: ADR-0055.

## Who and what

- **Eligible stories:** `analyzed`, `selected` and `published` (`RESEARCHABLE_STATUSES`, `services/researchInput.ts`) for an editor; the research step takes `selected` stories only. Any other status gets 409. A story with an eligible status that the loader returns nothing for (no title, summary or rating) gets 409 "This story lacks the analysis research needs", before any reservation is written.
- **One run per story at a time.** A second start while one runs gets 409.
- **The switch is the key:** `TAVILY_API_KEY` present means on (`config.research.tavilyApiKey`, read at call time). Removing it in Render switches research off: no run starts and none is applied, the research step and its publish gate stop, while a revert still works ("Applying a run", which also has the owner's separate gate on applying, `RESEARCH_APPLY_ENABLED`); the section then says so and the buttons stay visible, disabled with that reason. A step whose model has no price in `config.research.modelPrices` refuses every run, so an `OPENAI_MODEL_*` override can never run uncounted.

## What a run does

`researchStory(story, deps)` (`services/researchPipeline.ts`) does no I/O of its own; production gives it `createRunMeter`'s `call`, `tavily` and `admit` (`services/researchCalls.ts`). Steps, with their tier (`STEP_TIERS`): neighbour confirmation with the dedup prompt (small, skipped when the story has no neighbours), queries (small), Tavily news search (advanced depth, 8 results a query, 14 days before to 7 after the article's date, never past the run time: production passes it as `latestDate`), publishers' opt-outs and the deterministic source filter (`services/researchFilter.ts`), extraction per source and our earlier coverage (small, in parallel), the write step (medium), the citation and copying checks in code and then a fact check (small), up to two repair rounds that rewrite the failing items alone (the `rewrite` step, medium, each with a fact check of those items; "Repair" below), and the gate. **The write step never reads a source's text, the original article's included**: it reads the facts extracted from each (the article's are source `S0`), so every source wording the writer sees is also in front of both copying checks ("Own words" below). Giving it the sources' full texts instead was considered and rejected (owner decision 2026-11-13, ADR-0057). **The write step also tags the story it writes with an emotion, in the same call** (owner decision 2026-11-14): `emotionTag` in `writeSchema`, the pre-assessment's four tags with their definitions (`EMOTION_TAGS`, `EMOTION_TAG_DEFINITIONS`; the prompt carries `EMOTION_TAGS_PROMPT_BLOCK`), judged on how the researched story affects readers, so it may differ from the single-source article's tag where the facts show the development in another light. It is stored with the draft in every run, shadow included (`result.draft.emotionTag`; runs stored before 2026-11-14 have none); no check reads it, and the repair and derive rounds never change it (`withRepairs` and `withDerived` keep the draft's other fields). The checks run on every draft, item by item (`draftItems`: `Title`, `Story paragraph N`, `Why This Matters N`, `Caveat N`, `Summary`, `Blurb`, the labels the problems, the repairs and the drops key on; the title label, a topic of 1-3 words that states no claim, is not an item, and the key quote is one for the fact check alone, its last, `Key quote`: "The key quote" under "Own words"). Story paragraphs and bullets carry citations, and a cited item is judged against its cited facts plus the other facts of the same sources (`evidenceFor`), since readers see one citation per source; each source's publication date is a fact too (`publishedFact`, id `S2F0`), so a paragraph may date an event its source dates by weekday. **The title, summary and blurb cite nothing, yet are judged against every fact of the pool** (ADR-0057's amendment "headline, summary and blurb", 2026-11-13; `checkedItems` gives them the whole pool as evidence, `againstPool`): checked for copying only before, a researched title, "Scott Colom becomes Mississippi's first Black senator", passed in production although every fact said "the first since Reconstruction". A claim in them is supported only when some fact states it with the same scope, and a claim that drops a qualifier its fact gives ("since Reconstruction", "nearly", "projected") is unsupported, a rule the prompt states for every item. The prompt shows the pool once (`<POOL>`: every fact with its statement, `MEASURES` and `SUPPORT`, the sources', our earlier stories' and the date facts) and marks these three `<AGAINST_POOL/>` instead of repeating it; a recheck that holds none of them has no `<POOL>`. **The code fails only a citation's structure** (`checkCitations`): an id that is not among the facts, and a story paragraph that cites nothing. **A number it does not find fails nothing by itself** (owner decision 2026-11-13, ADR-0057): a number an item states that its evidence does not state digit for digit (`checkedItems`; for a bullet that cites nothing, every number it states; for the title, summary and blurb, a number no fact of the pool states) is a hint for the fact check on that item (`<NUMBERS_NOT_FOUND>`), whose rules count a number given in other words, rounded or in another unit ("half" for 50%, 22,000 MW for 22 GW) as stated; its verdict decides, and a bullet that cites nothing (one that only judges) has no evidence and is judged for support only once it carries a hint. Each pass records the hints (`numberHints`). A story paragraph or bullet still failing after repair is dropped and listed (`dropFailing`), and so is a key quote, which never ends the run; the run falls back (`draft_failed_check`, the item in `result.failedOn`) when an item the draft cannot do without still fails (`UNDROPPABLE`: `Story paragraph 1`, which carries the what, who, where and when, and the `Title`, `Summary` and `Blurb`; the reason says "a key claim" when the fact check judged its unsupported claim key, the main development itself), or when a part (story, Why This Matters, Caveats) is left empty: by the drops (`failedOn` its first item), or because the draft had nothing in it, even a draft that passed its checks (`failedOn` the part: `Story`, `Why This Matters`, `Caveats`; no repair is asked for it, since none can give the draft that part). **Only those four items can be key** (the owner's rule: only the main development is key, and a later paragraph, a bullet or a caveat never states it; held in code since 2026-11-13, after a shadow run fell back on a key `Why This Matters 3`): the fact-check prompt and the `key` schema description say so, and a `key` the judge still gives another item stays on its problem in the run and counts for nothing, so that item is dropped as any failing one. Every string of a parsed model answer is XML-unescaped (`unescapeXmlDeep`), since the model copies the prompts' escapes into what it writes. The gate (`decideGate`, `services/researchChecks.ts`) decides in this order: `stale` (the draft finds the news old), `contradicted` (other reports contradict its key claim), `research-rating` (the draft's own rating is below the publish bar, `config.selection.relevanceMin`), else `keep`. **`research-rating` is recorded only** (owner decision 2026-11-12): it rejects nothing, the story keeps its own rating, and the research step treats it as `keep` ("The pipeline step"); the order puts it last so a low-rated draft that is also stale or contradicted still gets that verdict. It never throws: a failed run comes back as `fallback` with its reason and its category ("Fallback reasons" below).

### Repair

**After the first check, only the failing items are rewritten, and every item that passed is frozen** (owner decision 2026-11-13, ADR-0058; `repair` in `researchPipeline.ts`, the pure targets and merge in `researchRepair.ts`). It replaced the one rewrite of the whole draft, which could break an item that had passed. Up to `config.research.repairRounds` rounds (2, no env override), each one `rewrite` call (`buildRepairPrompt`, `repairSchema`) and one fact-check call:

- **The repair prompt** shows the story as it stands, item by item (`storyItems`), all the facts, and in `<REPAIRS>` the failing items alone (`repairTargets`): each with its text, the ids it cites and every problem the checks found in it, as recorded (the judge's reason or the code's problem); the key quote with its attribution and source. It asks for the smallest fix that leaves none of the problems, with the write prompt's own-words, citation and key-quote rules. The answer gives, per label, the new text and fact ids, and a new key quote (`quote`, `quoteAttribution`, `quoteSourceId`) when the key quote failed.
- **The merge** (`withRepairs`) takes the answer for the failing labels alone: a label not asked for, a second answer for a label, an empty text and an item the same as before count for nothing, so an item that passed is never changed, byte for byte, whatever the answer holds (`researchRepair.test.ts` and the pipeline test fence it). A cited item takes the answer's fact ids; the title, summary and blurb cite nothing.
- **The recheck** runs the code checks and one fact-check call on the rewritten items alone (`check` with its `labels`; no call when nothing is left to judge, as for a key quote naming no source of the run). Its pass is recorded with `rechecked`, and each rechecked item's problems become that pass's (`afterRecheck`). A round whose answer changes nothing ends the rounds. **`result.rewritten` means a repair was asked for**: true once a repair round's or the derived fields' call ran, whatever it answered (empty, unchanged or unparseable included); a call the budget skipped does not count.
- **After the last round** a paragraph or bullet still failing is dropped, an item the draft cannot do without still failing ends the run, and a key quote still failing is dropped, as in "What a run does". **A title, summary or blurb still failing on copying alone** is first written again from the kept story paragraphs alone (the `derive` step, medium: `buildDerivePrompt`, `deriveSchema`, the write schema's three fields, of which only the failing ones are taken; its prompt holds no source's words), only when nothing else ends the run, and checked once more; still failing, the run falls back. One failing on a fact (a claim no fact of the pool states, "What a run does") is never written again: it goes through the repair rounds as any failing item, whose citation rules have it state only what facts state, with their qualifiers, and still failing after them, the run falls back.
- **The budget:** repair and derive calls count against the run's ceiling and the month's cap as every call does (`STEP_OUTPUT_TOKENS`: `rewrite` still sized for a whole draft, so it reserves more than a repair needs; `derive` 1,500). A repair or derive call the budget skips ends the repair, and so does a skipped recheck, whose repaired text is never kept: the drops and fallbacks then apply to what the last check saw. A skip of the first check still ends the run as `fallback: budget`, and a failed answer (a parse failure) ends it as `technical`, as the rewrite's did.
- **Worst case per run:** two repair rounds and the derived fields, three medium-tier calls and three fact checks, against one rewrite and one fact check before; a repair writes only the failing items, so its output is smaller than a whole draft's. Not yet measured: `eval:research` shows the calls per step.

### Own words

**A researched story is written in our own words and cited per paragraph** (owner decision 2026-11-13): a close translation of a source still reuses its expression, and attribution does not license it (press publishers' right and copyright). The write prompt asks for a short synthesis that never translates or restates a source's sentence or a fact's statement and never follows one source's order (`<OWN_WORDS>`, `prompts/research.ts`), and has no quotation marks outside the key quote; the extraction prompt has each fact's `statement` restate the fact neutrally in its own order and phrasing, never translate its supporting sentence (which stays verbatim, for `supportFound`). Two checks hold it, on every draft and every item:

- **The code check** (`checkCopying`, `researchCopying.ts`, the copying engine with `sourceLines`, `wordTokens` and `quotedSpans`; pure, free, deterministic): for each item and each outside source (`S…`; our earlier stories `E…` are our own text), the longest run of words the item shares with any one of that source's lines (`sourceLines`: kind `wording`, its title, each fact's supporting sentence and each voice's quote; kind `statement`, each fact's statement), each line compared on its own, so a run never crosses from one line or source into the next. A run of `config.research.copiedRunWords` (12, no env override) or more is a problem (`kind: 'copying'`, `by: 'code'`, its `sourceId` and `against`, the run quoted). Shorter runs collide with the scope rule, which has a figure repeated as its fact states it ("22 GW of rooftop solar in two years"); a lower bar is the legal reviewer's call, read against `longestRuns`. A quoted span of 4 words or more in any item is a problem too: a person's words go only in the key quote, which this check never reads.
- **Tokens** (`wordTokens`, used for runs and spans alike): NFKC, soft hyphens removed, lowercase; a token is a run of letters (with their combining marks) or of digits, or a `%` or currency symbol alone, so a letter-digit boundary splits ("22GW" and "22 GW" both give `22`, `gw`); digit groups join ("1,200", "1.200" and "1 200" give `1200`; "3.5" gives `35`, an equality key only); an apostrophe inside a word goes ("Pakistan's" gives `pakistans`); hyphens, dashes, quote marks and all other punctuation separate. **Quoted spans** (`quotedSpans`) run from an opening mark to the nearest closing mark of the same family (double `“ ” „ " « »`, single `‘ ’ ‚ ' ‹ ›`), so „…“, „…”, «…», »…« and “…” count; a straight `'` opens only at a word's start and closes only at its end, and an apostrophe inside a word never opens or closes one.
- **The judge** is the fact-check call itself, no new call: its prompt shows every outside source's own words once, in their original language (`<SOURCE_WORDING>`: titles `S2T`, supporting sentences `S2F3`, quotes `S2Q1` each with its `speaker`, the voice's name and role, from `sourceLines`), then every fact once if the call holds the title, summary or blurb (`<POOL>`), then every item with its cited facts, the `<AGAINST_POOL/>` mark or neither, and its number hints if it has any (`<ITEMS>`, "What a run does"). **Its standard is expression, not facts** (owner decision 2026-11-13, ADR-0057's amendment "copying standard"): copying is reproducing a source's expression, its distinctive phrasing, a sentence structure the facts do not dictate, or its sequence of points across several sentences, in nearly the same words or as a close translation from any language. Plainly stating a fact with its names, figures, units, period and scope is not copying, even in the source's order and close to its words: there are few ways to say it, and facts are not protected. The anchor, a false positive of the judge before this standard: "Countries raised domestic tuberculosis funding by 14% to $5.6 billion between 2024 and 2025, partly making up for falling international support." against WHO's "domestic funding for TB increased by 14% to US$ 5.6 billion between 2024 and 2025, in part offsetting a decline in international financing of a similar scale." is not copying, and the code check passes it too (`researchCopying.test.ts`). The prompt carries a Not copying / Copying pair of its own on the same pattern, not this case. Per item it answers, beside `supported`, `problem` and `key`, `copiedSentence` (the line whose expression one of the item's sentences reproduces) and `followsSource` (the one source whose points several of the item's sentences follow in its order and phrasing while the item draws on other sources too). **Code ignores `followsSource` on an item whose cited facts all come from one source** (`citesOneSource`, a source's date fact `S2F0` counting as that source): such an item follows that source's order by nature. An item that cites nothing (the title, summary and blurb) can still follow one. Sharing names, figures, units, places and technical terms is not copying, and a passage that merges several sources gets no exemption: each sentence is judged on its own. **The code check stays the hard limit**, unchanged: a run of 12 words fails whatever the judge says. A flag becomes a problem with `kind: 'copying'`, `by: 'judge'` and its `sourceId`, never key (`judgedProblems`): `renders S2F3 ("<the source's sentence>") closely`, quoting the line in its original language so the repair sees what it echoed, or "follows S2's order and wording". An id the prompt did not give counts for nothing, and so does a support verdict on a bullet that cites nothing and carries no number hint (the prompt says such an item is supported); on the title, summary and blurb it counts, since their evidence is the pool. The write prompt names each voice's quote by the same id (`<QUOTE id="S2Q1">`, `quoteId`), as the facts carry theirs, so a flag on a quote points at text the writer saw under that id. **An item the answer leaves out** (no entry with its number) is recorded in the pass's `unjudged` and fails nothing: the code checks still ran on it.
- **On a flag:** the repair rounds ("Repair"), whose rules ask for a copied item's facts in our own words, not in the source's phrasing, structure or order and not lightly reworded; an item still flagged after them is dropped, or the run falls back, as any failing item ("What a run does"), a title, summary or blurb only after it was written again from the kept story.
- **The key quote** (`quote`, `quoteAttribution`, `quoteSourceId`; owner decision 2026-11-13, ADR-0057's amendment; `researchQuote.ts`) is the one place a source's exact words belong, so **neither copying check flags it**: `checkCopying` skips it, and the judge's `copiedSentence` and `followsSource` on it count for nothing. The fact check judges it as its last item (`checkedQuote`), with its `ATTRIBUTION` and `QUOTED_SOURCE`, against that source's quotes and supporting sentences in `<SOURCE_WORDING>`: faithful is the same words or their English translation, the same meaning, and the speaker the source gives them to; context in square brackets ("[the ministry]"), an ellipsis for words left out and differences of quote marks, punctuation or case are faithful. Code judges its words only as a hint (`<QUOTE_NOT_FOUND/>`, `quoteFound`): the quote, split at each bracketed insertion and ellipsis ("[T]he" stays a word), does not stand in order in one of those lines as `wordTokens` reads them. A translation never stands there, so the hint fails nothing by itself and is not recorded. **A `quoteSourceId` that is not one of the run's sources** (`S…`; an earlier story's `E1` or a quote's `S2Q1` included) fails it in code (`quoteProblems`, `kind: 'fact'`, `by: 'code'`), and the judge does not see it. A failure goes into the repair rounds as any problem does (they ask for a quote taken again from a source's words, with its speaker and source); **a key quote still failing after them is dropped, never the run** (`withoutFailedQuote`: the three fields emptied; its problem is never key) and listed in `result.problems` under `Key quote`, as a dropped paragraph is. An apply then writes no quote (`researchedValues` stores null, the projection's `quoteSourceId` is null), and the story page, cards, newsletter and feeds show none, as for any story without one. One the answer leaves out is `unjudged` and fails nothing. The write prompt asks for words of a SOURCE, never an EARLIER story, kept as the source has them. Its problems count under `fact` in the tallies below; the last query counts them apart.
- **Recorded in the run:** each problem's cause (`kind` `fact` or `copying`, `by` `code` or `judge`, and for copying its `sourceId` with `against` or `copiedSentence`); `result.checks`, one entry per pass (the first draft's, then one per repair round and one for the derived fields; runs before ADR-0058 have one, or two after the whole-draft rewrite), each `{ problems, longestRuns, unjudged, numberHints }` and, on a recheck, `rechecked`, the labels it checked again (the first pass checks every item and has none), where `longestRuns` holds each item's longest run per outside source and kind (`{ label, sourceId, against, words }`), below the threshold too, `unjudged` the labels of the items the fact check gave no verdict for, and `numberHints` each item with numbers the code did not find in its evidence (`{ label, numbers }`; runs stored before ADR-0057 have none); and `result.failedOn`, the item a `draft_failed_check` fallback failed on, or the part's name for a part the draft had nothing in (else null). `problems` keeps its meaning: what the last check of each item found, the last pass that checked it. Runs stored before 2026-11-13 have none of these.
- **Cost:** the shared block adds about 2.5k input tokens to each fact-check call (some 6 sources of 8 sentences, 4 quotes and a title), about $0.0003 a call on the small tier, at most four calls a run (the first pass, two repair rounds and the derived fields; the key quote's item and the quotes' speakers add about 100 tokens more); the write and repair prompts lose the article's text. `<POOL>` adds every fact once to the first pass and to a recheck that holds the title, summary or blurb: some 45 facts, about 4k input tokens, $0.0005 a call (not yet measured). A key quote that fails alone costs a repair round like any other problem. The repair rate is the cost to watch: a repair round is a medium-tier call, though it writes only the failing items. `STEP_OUTPUT_TOKENS` stays until `eval:research` has measured the new output.
- **Applied stories from before are live**: the owner has applied researched versions to published stories by hand since applying opened on 2026-11-13 ("Applying a run"). They keep their per-sentence citations and were never checked for copying; nothing migrates them. The owner researches each again and applies the new run, or reverts it (`ai-transparency.md`, §11); `SELECT id, slug FROM stories WHERE pre_research IS NOT NULL`, run before this change deploys, lists them.

**The shadow week's tally** is read-only SQL over the runs (no endpoint; `eval:research` counts the same in its report, `scripts/eval/research/ownWords.ts`). Each counts the last 7 days; runs from before 2026-11-13 have no `checks` and add nothing. Every cause carries the same `detail`, which keeps the code's runs shared with a fact's English **statement** apart from those shared with the source's own **wording**: the write prompt has a figure keep its fact's exact scope ("22 GW of rooftop solar in two years"), so statement flags well above wording flags point at that rule, not at copying from the source:

```sql
-- First-draft problems by cause, and the runs they touch
SELECT p->>'kind' AS kind, p->>'by' AS found_by,
       CASE WHEN p->>'kind' = 'fact' THEN NULL
            WHEN p->>'by' = 'code' THEN COALESCE(p->>'against', 'quoted span')
            WHEN p ? 'copiedSentence' THEN 'close rendering'
            ELSE 'follows a source' END AS detail,
       COUNT(*) AS problems, COUNT(DISTINCT r.id) AS runs
FROM story_research_runs r, jsonb_array_elements(r.result->'checks'->0->'problems') p
WHERE r.started_at > now() - interval '7 days'
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3 ORDER BY 5 DESC;

-- The repair rate (rewritten: a repair round, or before ADR-0058 the whole-draft rewrite, was asked for)
SELECT COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE (result->>'rewritten')::boolean) AS rewritten, COUNT(*) AS checked
FROM story_research_runs WHERE started_at > now() - interval '7 days' AND result ? 'checks';

-- Paragraphs, bullets and key quotes dropped from kept drafts, by cause
SELECT p->>'kind' AS kind, p->>'by' AS found_by,
       CASE WHEN p->>'kind' = 'fact' THEN NULL
            WHEN p->>'by' = 'code' THEN COALESCE(p->>'against', 'quoted span')
            WHEN p ? 'copiedSentence' THEN 'close rendering'
            ELSE 'follows a source' END AS detail,
       COUNT(DISTINCT r.id::text || '/' || (p->>'label')) AS items
FROM story_research_runs r, jsonb_array_elements(r.result->'problems') p
WHERE r.started_at > now() - interval '7 days' AND r.verdict <> 'fallback' AND r.result ? 'checks'
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3 ORDER BY 4 DESC;

-- draft_failed_check fallbacks by the cause of the item they failed on; a part the draft had
-- nothing in (failedOn 'Story', 'Why This Matters' or 'Caveats') has no problem and counts as 'empty part'
SELECT r.result->>'failedOn' AS item, p->>'kind' AS kind, p->>'by' AS found_by,
       CASE WHEN p IS NULL THEN 'empty part'
            WHEN p->>'kind' = 'fact' THEN NULL
            WHEN p->>'by' = 'code' THEN COALESCE(p->>'against', 'quoted span')
            WHEN p ? 'copiedSentence' THEN 'close rendering'
            ELSE 'follows a source' END AS detail,
       COUNT(DISTINCT r.id) AS runs
FROM story_research_runs r
LEFT JOIN LATERAL jsonb_array_elements(r.result->'problems') p ON p->>'label' = r.result->>'failedOn'
WHERE r.started_at > now() - interval '7 days' AND r.result->'verdict'->>'category' = 'draft_failed_check'
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3, 4 ORDER BY 5 DESC;

-- Items the fact check gave no verdict for, per pass (1 the first draft, then each repair round and
-- the derived fields' check, which hold the rechecked items alone; 2 the whole-draft rewrite before ADR-0058)
SELECT pass.n AS pass, COUNT(*) AS items, COUNT(DISTINCT r.id) AS runs
FROM story_research_runs r,
     jsonb_array_elements(r.result->'checks') WITH ORDINALITY AS pass(c, n),
     jsonb_array_elements_text(pass.c->'unjudged') u
WHERE r.started_at > now() - interval '7 days'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;

-- Items with number hints (numbers the code did not find in their evidence), per pass, by the
-- fact check's verdict on the same item in the same pass; 'passed' is the judge accepting a
-- reworded, rounded or converted number the code could not match
SELECT pass.n AS pass,
       CASE WHEN EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM jsonb_array_elements(pass.c->'problems') p
                         WHERE p->>'label' = h->>'label' AND p->>'kind' = 'fact' AND p->>'by' = 'judge') THEN 'failed'
            WHEN pass.c->'unjudged' ? (h->>'label') THEN 'no verdict'
            ELSE 'passed' END AS judged,
       COUNT(*) AS items, COUNT(DISTINCT r.id) AS runs
FROM story_research_runs r,
     jsonb_array_elements(r.result->'checks') WITH ORDINALITY AS pass(c, n),
     jsonb_array_elements(pass.c->'numberHints') h
WHERE r.started_at > now() - interval '7 days'
GROUP BY 1, 2 ORDER BY 1, 2;

-- Each first-draft item's longest shared run, per kind
SELECT against, words, COUNT(*) AS items FROM (
  SELECT r.id, l->>'label' AS label, l->>'against' AS against, MAX((l->>'words')::int) AS words
  FROM story_research_runs r, jsonb_array_elements(r.result->'checks'->0->'longestRuns') l
  WHERE r.started_at > now() - interval '7 days'
  GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
) longest GROUP BY 1, 2 ORDER BY 1, 2;

-- Key quotes failed, per pass (1 the first draft, then each repair round): 'code' names no source of
-- the run, 'judge' found it unfaithful; in a kept run, a failure in the last pass that rechecked the
-- key quote (or in pass 1 when none did) is a dropped key quote
SELECT pass.n AS pass, p->>'by' AS found_by, COUNT(DISTINCT r.id) AS runs
FROM story_research_runs r,
     jsonb_array_elements(r.result->'checks') WITH ORDINALITY AS pass(c, n),
     jsonb_array_elements(pass.c->'problems') p
WHERE r.started_at > now() - interval '7 days' AND p->>'label' = 'Key quote'
GROUP BY 1, 2 ORDER BY 1, 2;
```

### Fallback reasons

**Every `fallback` verdict records why, as one of four categories** (`FALLBACK_CATEGORIES`, `researchChecks.ts`), stored in the run's result (`result.verdict.category`). It is recording only: no verdict and no behavior depends on it. The category comes from where the run stopped (`Stop`, `researchPipeline.ts`):

- **`no_other_coverage`:** no other report was left to read once publishers' opt-outs and the source filter had run (the search found nothing, or nothing on the story's dates and outlets the filter takes).
- **`not_confirmed`:** other reports passed the filter and the extraction read at least one of them, yet fewer than two sources were kept: the others were off-topic, unusable, or a press release (`keepExtracted`). This is the stop after extraction, told apart from the one before it by whether any other source's extraction came back.
- **`draft_failed_check`:** the draft failed its checks after repair ("Repair"; the whole-draft rewrite before ADR-0058): an item the draft cannot do without (the first story paragraph, the title, the summary, the blurb) still failed, a key claim in it among them (a key judgment on any other item counts for nothing), or dropping the failing paragraphs and bullets left a part empty (`dropFailing`); or the draft had nothing in a part, checked or not; `result.failedOn` names the item, or the part.
- **`technical`:** everything else: the monthly budget or the run's ceiling, a step's failed answer (`queries: parse_failure`, say), a Tavily error, every other source's extraction failing, an unexpected error.

The run view names it ("Today's format (no other coverage): no usable source."), as does the run picker; `GET /usage` counts this UTC month's fallbacks by category, both triggers, as `fallbacksThisMonth`, and the Research section lists them. Runs from before the category have none: they count as `unrecorded` and show as "Today's format".

- **The story's material** (`researchStoriesByIds`): the story, its cluster members, up to six assessed stories (analyzed, selected, published or rejected) crawled within 14 days either side and outside its cluster, nearest by embedding distance, which the dedup prompt confirms, and up to six published stories from the 12 months before it, also by embedding distance. A story not yet published is dated at the run time (`COALESCE(date_published, now() AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')` in SQL, matching the columns' UTC timestamps). Without an embedding it gets no neighbours or earlier coverage and still searches.
- **Outlet names:** a source found by search has only its address, so it is shown under the name the extraction read from its page (`outlet` in `extractionSchema`, whitespace collapsed, at most 60 characters), else under its domain without `www.` (`domainOf`). The original article and our own sources keep their feed's name. The name is untrusted model output from a fetched page, rendered as text only.
- **Steps run on the tier models** (`getLLMByTier`), each after `rateLimitDelay()`, never inside `withRetry` (the SDK retries; `llm-analysis.md`, "Retries"). Tavily is plain HTTP and goes through `withRetry` (`lib/tavily.ts`).

## Cap and reservation

- **Monthly cap:** `RESEARCH_MONTHLY_CAP_USD` (whole USD, default 30). The month's spend is the sum of `cost_usd` over all runs started this UTC month, deleted stories' runs and running reservations included.
- **Reservation:** a run is inserted with `cost_usd = config.research.maxRunUsd` ($0.25) in one transaction with the in-progress and cap checks, under the advisory lock `RESEARCH_RESERVATION_LOCK` (its own key, not the TTS one). It is refused when the spend plus the reservation would pass the cap. Once the research ends, the measured cost replaces the reservation, stored with the result before the run finishes (and before the research step applies its verdict). The research step's runs reserve the same way, under the same lock and cap; refused at the cap, the step records a $0 run instead ("The pipeline step").
- **During the run:** before every model call and every search, and before the parallel extraction batch against the batch's estimate, the meter checks the room left: the smaller of the run's own ceiling ($0.25) and the cap minus every other run's spend this month. A call without room is `skipped`, a search without room throws `BudgetExhaustedError`, and a batch without room is refused; each ends the run as `fallback: budget`. Estimates are desk constants (`STEP_OUTPUT_TOKENS`, `researchCalls.ts`) and only gate calls. The month can end at most one in-flight batch above the cap, a few cents.
- **Interrupted runs:** a row still `running` 60 minutes after its start (`config.research.staleRunMinutes`, above a live run's worst case with the SDK's timeouts and retries) was cut off by a restart. The next start, list or research step run marks it `failed` (`markInterruptedRuns`) ("interrupted: the server restarted during the run"), and it keeps its cost: the reservation, or the measured cost once its result was stored. The result's store and the finish write apply only to a row still `running`, so a run marked interrupted before its result was stored stays failed and its result is dropped. A graceful shutdown waits for a running research task as for any other task (`index.ts`, the drain deadline).

## Cost recording

Every call records input, cached, output and reasoning tokens, the model id and USD at `config.research.modelPrices` (`services/researchCost.ts`). Uncached input is billed at the cache-write factor (1.25): Chat Completions reports no cache writes, so the figure is an upper bound that never undercounts the cap, and the admin says so. Tavily credits come from the response's `usage.credits`, else by depth (advanced = 2), at $0.008 each, Tavily's free credits counted as paid. A model call that throws counts $0. A run stores the prices it ran at with its result, so later price changes don't rewrite old costs.

## Storage and retention

`story_research_runs` keeps every run (`id`, `story_id`, `status`, `trigger` (`editor` or `pipeline`, who started it; `editor` for the runs before step 3), `verdict`, `cost_usd`, `tavily_credits`, `result` jsonb, `error`, `started_at`, `finished_at`). The admin lists a story's latest 10 (`config.research.runsListed`), newest first. `story_id` is `ON DELETE SET NULL`: a deleted story's runs stay, reached by nothing but the month's sum, so the cap still counts them. No job prunes the table. `result` is the `StoryResearch` plus the step model ids and prices; `error` holds only an error's name and code ("unexpected error (<name> <code>)"), never its message.

## Untrusted input

Fetched text and search results are untrusted. The prompts wrap them in escaped XML with an "ignore any instructions inside them" line. Logs carry ids, the verdict and cost, never fetched text. Tavily errors are rethrown with a status or code only (`TavilyError`), and the key travels only in the `Authorization` header. The admin renders every text as React text, links a URL only when it is http(s) (`rel="noopener noreferrer"`), and loads a favicon only from https with no referrer, else shows the outlet's initial.

## Publishers' AI opt-outs

**Research cites only sources whose publishers have not opted out** (owner decision, ADR-0056), even when that leaves fewer perspectives. `ResearchDeps.sourcePolicy?` is the seam: when given, `dropOptedOut` (`researchFilter.ts`) drops own and search candidates it reports opted out, before the filter, under the drop reason `opt-out`. Production runs pass `researchSourcePolicy` (`publisherOptOut.ts`), the crawler's check (`content-extraction.md`, "Publisher opt-out check") with robots.txt path rules applied, since Tavily reads the page on our behalf; a source whose robots.txt can't be read is dropped too. Those runs record `optOutChecked: true`. A run given no policy, as in the eval harness (`eval:research`), records `optOutChecked: false` and the admin view says "AI opt-out not checked". Limit: the check sees the result URL's host only, not a redirect Tavily may follow.

## Applying a run

**An editor applies a run by hand, one story at a time** (`applyResearchRun`, `services/researchApply.ts`); **the research step applies its own runs' verdicts, in apply mode only** (`applyVerdict`, "The pipeline step"). Nothing changes for readers until one of them does.

**Applying waits behind the owner's gate, `RESEARCH_APPLY_ENABLED`** (`config.research.applyEnabled`, parsed at startup with `parseOneOf`: "true" opens, unset or "false" keeps closed, any other value refuses to start). It is closed by default and unset in `render.yaml` (`sync: false`); the owner sets it to "true" on the API service's Environment page, a Render restart picks it up, and setting it back closes it again. **It is open in production since 2026-11-13**: the owner applies researched versions to published stories by hand, so researched text reaches readers (versions applied before the own-words change cite per sentence, "Own words"). While it is closed, an apply answers 409 with that reason and "Use this version" stays visible but disabled with it, while research runs and reverts work as usual. `applyRefusal()` gives the reason (the missing key first, then the gate), and `GET /usage` serves it as `applyRefusal`.

- **Storage, a swap with a snapshot** (plan, "Storage"): an apply writes the run's researched texts into the story's own columns (`researchedValues`, `services/researchSwap.ts`): `title`, `titleLabel`, `summary` (the draft's short summary), `quote`, `quoteAttribution`, `marketingBlurb`, `relevanceReasons` and `antifactors` (the bullets as `- text` lines, as assess writes them) and `emotionTag`, the tag the write step gave the researched story (owner decision 2026-11-14), with `relevanceSummary` set to null. A run stored before 2026-11-14 has no tag (`appliedDraftSchema` takes it as optional): its apply keeps the story's tag. **The rating is never swapped** (owner decision 2026-11-12): `relevance` and `relevanceCalculation` keep the assess step's values, so the story's rating stays the one it was selected on, on the public page, in the admin and in every list; the draft's own rating stays in the run (`result.draft.rating`, the run view's "Research's rating … (recorded; the story keeps its own)") for evaluation. A snapshot an apply stored before this rule may still hold the rating; it is read without it, so its revert leaves the rating as it is. Every reader of those columns (cards, homepage, RSS, API lists, embed and widget, newsletter, social, the og image and link previews, carousel, search and the embedding) then takes the researched text with no change of its own; where a reader took `relevanceSummary`, it falls back to the summary (cards, embed), the blurb (newsletter body) or the bullets (podcast). The emotion tag's readers (the public mood filter and the homepage's mood sections, the Actually Uplifting newsletter and podcast, the social posts' metadata line, the selection prompts) read it at query time, so they take research's tag the same way. `stories.research` (jsonb) holds the cited version the story page shows; `stories.pre_research` (jsonb) every value the apply replaced, `relevanceSummary` included. Both are null on a single-source story.
- **The snapshot is written only when empty**, so applying a second run keeps the first snapshot and a revert always returns to the single-source analysis. A snapshot an apply stored before 2026-11-14 holds no emotion tag, since that apply left the tag alone: the next apply first completes it with the story's tag, still its own then (`snapshotForApply`), and until then a revert leaves the tag as it is.
- **Which runs can be applied:** a completed run of this story with a draft whose verdict is not `fallback`. A `research-rating` run applies as a kept one does, and a run research rejected (`stale`, `contradicted`) can be applied too: the editor overrules it (owner decision 2026-11-12; publishing such a story without applying gives today's format). Refusals, in order: 409 research switched off, 409 the owner's gate closed, 404 unknown story, 404 unknown run, 409 a run of another story, not completed, without a draft, fallen back, or with a draft an older write step stored without a field the apply needs or in an older shape (`appliedDraftSchema`; a draft from before 2026-11-13, whose paragraphs are lists of cited sentences, was never written or checked for our own words and is refused so, "research the story again"), then 409 for the story's status, and 409 when the run's sources make no projection that passes `researchedStorySchema`.
- **Statuses:** the lifecycle's `research` stage (`storyLifecycle.ts`) writes `analyzed`, `selected`, `published` and `rejected` stories in place and never moves a status. A trashed story gets 409 "restore it first"; `fetched` and `pre_analyzed` get 409.
- **The write:** a fresh embedding of the written title, label and summary, made before the transaction, then `recordAnalysis('research', 'editor', …)`, fenced on the status and on `updatedAt` as read before the embedding call (the row's `where`). A story whose status changed, or that anything wrote in between (an editor's save during the embedding call, whose values the snapshot would otherwise miss and the apply overwrite), gets 409 "The story changed while this was written; reload it and try again", and keeps what was written meanwhile. `recordAnalysis` forgets a refreshed story's related-stories cache. The research step's apply shares this write (`putResearch`) as the `pipeline` actor, fenced on `selected` and the `updatedAt` read before its run started, and skips such a story instead of answering 409.
- **Favicons:** after the write the cited outlets' favicons are fetched in the background (`fetchSourceFaviconsInBackground`, below); the page shows each outlet's initial until its icon is stored.
- **Revert** (`revertResearch`) writes the snapshot back, `relevanceSummary` and the emotion tag included, clears both columns and re-embeds, fenced on the status and `updatedAt` read now as an apply is; 409 without a snapshot, on a trashed story, and when the snapshot fails its schema (logged; a re-assess then replaces the researched version). The runs stay.
- **A re-assess clears both columns** (`assessStory`): it writes a fresh single-source analysis over the researched one, and takes the snapshot's emotion tag back (`snapshotEmotionTag`), since assess writes no tag of its own and research's would otherwise stay on a single-source story; its uplifting-headline rule then follows that tag. A snapshot without a tag leaves the tag as it is. Its write is fenced on the snapshot read before its model call (`sameSnapshot`), beside the status: a first apply, a revert or a snapshot an apply completed during the call skips it (logged), so research's version and the single-source tag in its snapshot are never cleared unread.
- **Re-screening and reclassifying keep research's tag:** pre-assessment, the bulk Reclassify and the emotion-only tagging judge the original article, so on a researched story they write their tag into the snapshot's `emotionTag` (`classifiedTag`), which a revert restores, and leave the column to research's tag; fenced on the snapshot read like a re-assess (`llm-analysis.md`, "Reclassification").
- **Edit lock:** while `pre_research` is set, `updateStory` refuses with 409 "Revert to single-source first: …" an edit that changes one of the swapped columns (`SWAPPED_FIELDS`, `relevanceSummary` and `emotionTag` among them). The edit form sends every field, so the check compares values: an unchanged field, other fields and status moves save as before. The form disables the swapped fields, the Emotion select among them, and says why; the rating and its calculation are not swapped, so an editor can change them on a researched story too. When the story gains or loses its researched version while the form holds unsaved edits (an apply or a revert, then the refetch), the swapped fields take the story's new values and the other edits stay (`useResearchLock`), so a save never writes researched text back over a reverted story, nor stale single-source text into a researched one.
- **Public caches** (RSS, homepage, og image, link previews) catch up within their TTLs, as after an editor's edit.
- **The podcast** scripts a researched story from its story text (`storedResearchText`: the paragraphs joined, citations stripped) in place of its summary, and its "why it matters" falls back to the researched bullets (`podcastScriptStory`, `promptInputs.ts`). An episode already scripted keeps its text.
- **Switching research off** (removing `TAVILY_API_KEY`) stops new runs and new applies: an apply then answers 409 before anything else, and "Use this version" stays disabled with that reason. Closing the gate again stops new applies only, the research step's included. **Already-researched stories stay researched** (owner decision 2026-11-12), and a revert still works, so an editor takes them back one story at a time.

## The pipeline step

**The `research_stories` job researches the selected stories; only in apply mode does `publish_stories` wait for it, and then only for stories selected less than two hours ago.** It is seeded **disabled** (its migration and `seed-jobs.ts`), ordered between `select_stories` and `publish_stories`, and resumable (`RESUMABLE_JOBS`: a rerun skips the stories that have a pipeline run). **It runs once a day, on the day's selection: at 02:20 UTC (`20 2 * * *`), 15 minutes after `select_stories` (02:05), a margin for a selection slowed by model retries, and `publish_stories` runs at 02:45** (migration `20261112230000_research_daily_schedule`, which moves each row only where it still holds the old schedule, hourly `15 * * * *` for research and `15 2 * * *` for publish, so a schedule the owner changed stays; `seed-jobs.ts` keeps the order on its own times, research at 10:15 between select at 10:00 and publish at 11:00). A day's selection, about 4 to 11 stories, is researched `config.research.pipeline.concurrency` (4) at a time, a few minutes at most, well inside the 25 minutes before publishing. A story selected after the step's run (a `select_stories` still running at 02:20, or an editor's selection later in the day) waits for the next day's run: in apply mode the gate holds it at that day's 02:45 publish, and it goes out researched a day later; in shadow it publishes at 02:45 unresearched, as every story then does. Plan: `.plans/2026-11-12_researched-stories-reader.md`, "Pipeline step" and "Publish gate".

- **The step's state** (`researchStepState`, `services/researchQueue.ts`), read from the job row and the config: it **researches** while `TAVILY_API_KEY` is set and every step's model is priced, the job enabled or not; it **applies** only while it researches, the job row is enabled and the mode in effect is apply (`pipelineMode()`, "Modes" below). Only then are verdicts applied and does the publish gate hold.
- **Each run** (`jobs/researchStories.ts`) first marks stale runs failed (`markInterruptedRuns`), then, while research is on, takes up to `RESEARCH_PIPELINE_PER_RUN` (20) selected stories with no applied research and no pipeline run, oldest crawled first (`nextToResearch`), and researches them `concurrency` (4) at a time: each of that many workers takes the next story as its last one finishes. Before it starts a story a worker re-reads the step's state and, once research is switched off or the job it started enabled is disabled, starts no other story (runs already in flight finish); then it reads the story's last write (`owedStory`: still owed, else skipped, since an editor may have moved or researched it meanwhile).
- **A run of the disabled job is a trial** (the Jobs page's "Run now", which runs a disabled job): the disabled job cannot gate, so the run researches in shadow and **never applies, even if the job is enabled during it**, and publishing does not wait. Its runs settle their stories as any shadow run does. The owner can so try the step on a real selection without enabling it.
- **One story** (`runPipelineResearch`, `researchRuns.ts`): a run marked `trigger = 'pipeline'`, reserved against the monthly cap as an editor's is, researched in the job's own call (no `taskRegistry` task). Concurrent runs reserve one at a time under the advisory lock, each seeing the reservations before it, so four runs started together never pass the cap together: with room for two, two start and two record the $0 skipped run below. During a run, the meter counts every other run's spend, running reservations included, so the parallel runs share the month's room as they spend it. `executeRun` then **stores the run's result, verdict and measured cost while the row is still `running`**, calls the job's hook, and finishes the row. So the publish gate keeps the story waiting while the verdict is applied, and **a verdict never lands without its stored run**: a failure in the apply or the finish write leaves the run `failed` with its result and its measured cost.
- **The hook** applies the verdict only if the job was enabled at the run's start and the step still applies when the hook runs (re-read then, so disabling the job or closing the mode during a run applies nothing); otherwise the run is shadow.
- **Verdicts in apply mode** (`applyVerdict`): every write is **fenced on the story's status and `updatedAt` as read before its run started**, so an editor's move, edit or apply during the run always wins: the step then neither applies nor rejects, logs it, and the run stays a normal completed run an editor can still apply by hand. `keep` and `research-rating` write the researched version as an editor's apply does, as the `pipeline` actor, and the story keeps its own rating (**a rating below the bar rejects nothing**, owner decision 2026-11-12). **Only `stale` and `contradicted` reject** the story, through `transition(…, { to: 'rejected', reason }, 'pipeline', { where: { updatedAt } })` (the move's fence, `MoveFence`) with the reasons `stale` and `contradicted`, each allowed only from `selected` (`PIPELINE_REJECTION_FROM`). An editor can still apply such a run or publish the story by hand. The `research_rating` rejection reason stays in the database enum, unused: the pipeline has no move for it (no destructive migration). `fallback`, or a kept or `research-rating` run whose draft can't be applied, leaves the story single-source: it publishes in today's format. A research rejection, like every pipeline rejection, re-elects no cluster primary: the story's duplicates stay rejected. Any write to the story during the run skips the apply, a job's too (a dedup cluster assignment, say), erring toward the editor's state.
- **Modes:** `RESEARCH_PIPELINE_MODE` (`config.research.pipeline.mode`, `parseOneOf`: `shadow`, the default, or `apply`; unset in `render.yaml`, `sync: false`), read at startup, so a change takes a Render restart. **In `shadow`** the step researches and stores its runs, no verdict is applied, and **publishing does not wait for it**: stories publish on their schedule while shadow research runs alongside (owner decision 2026-11-12). **`apply` takes effect only while applying is open** (`applyRefusal()` null: the key set and `RESEARCH_APPLY_ENABLED` "true"); otherwise the step runs as shadow (`pipelineMode()`), logs a warning with the reason at each run's start, and the Jobs page shows "Shadow mode, apply held back (RESEARCH_APPLY_ENABLED is not "true"): publishing does not wait" (`jobSettings.ts`). `applyVerdict` checks `pipelineMode()` itself too, as a backstop to its caller.
- **At the cap, and for a story the loader can't research,** nothing is reserved: the step records a `failed` pipeline run at $0 ("skipped: monthly cap reached", "skipped: the story lacks the analysis research needs"), which settles the story, so it publishes single-source; the admin shows such a run as skipped, not failed. While an editor's run of the story is in progress, no row is written and the next run tries the story again.
- **Errors:** an unexpected error in the research fails that run at its reservation (its real cost is not known); one in the apply or the finish write fails it keeping its result and measured cost (sanitized error either way). An error outside a run (a database error reading the story or reserving) records a $0 skipped pipeline run, "skipped: unexpected error (<name>)" (`recordSkippedRun`), so the story settles; if even that write fails, it is logged and the gate's two-hour bound releases the story. Either way the next story runs, and the job does not fail.
- **The publish gate** (`awaitingResearch`, called by `publish_stories`): **only while the step applies**, the publish leaves out a selected story with no applied research and no settled pipeline run, **for at most `config.research.pipeline.maxWaitMinutes` (120) after it entered `selected`** (`stories.selected_at`, stamped by every lifecycle move into `selected`; a story with none, selected before the column existed and not backfilled, is never held). A completed or failed pipeline run settles it, and so does one still `running` past `staleRunMinutes` (60), so a deploy that cut a run off holds nothing. No run yet, or one running within that time, keeps it waiting until the bound, after which it publishes single-source whatever its runs: a published story leaves the queue, and a run in progress when it publishes applies nothing (the story moved). An editor's run settles nothing. An editor's publish never waits. The bound is a plain constant, independent of the research job's schedule. **It ends the hold, not the wait:** a story it releases publishes at the next `publish_stories` run, so with the daily publish (production 02:45, 25 minutes after the research step) a story still owed then waits for the next day's run, by which time that day's research step has taken it. A publish schedule more frequent than the bound (hourly, say) makes two hours the real longest wait; that schedule is the owner's call.
- **Kill switch:** disable `research_stories` on the Jobs page: a run starts no other story (at most the four stories being researched finish, and their verdicts are not applied) and the next publish no longer waits. Or set the mode to `shadow` and restart: research goes on, nothing is applied, nothing waits. Stories already researched or rejected stay so; an editor reverts or moves them one at a time.
- **Rollout** (owner steps): set `TAVILY_API_KEY`, try one run with "Run now" on the still-disabled job (a shadow trial), enable the job in shadow and watch runs and cost for a week (the SQL under "Own words": first-draft flags per run by cause, the repair rounds they cause and the paragraphs and bullets dropped, the `draft_failed_check` share against the week before split by cause, the longest-run distribution, which settles the 12-word threshold, the number hints by the fact check's verdict, cost per run; and for the legal reviewer a manual sample of about 10 kept drafts and 10 judge-flagged items read against their sources, for the judge's recall on translations and its precision, with examples of the copying standard: the tuberculosis anchor under "Own words" and kept items that restate a fact close to its source's words), run the design's evaluation (blind comparison, fact audit, rating drift with `eval:recalibrate`, where the research's own rating, which runs record and the story never takes, is the figure to compare; the fallback categories in the Research section say why runs fell back), then set `RESEARCH_PIPELINE_MODE=apply` (`RESEARCH_APPLY_ENABLED` is already open, for the owner's applies by hand). The methodology page's "Researched Stories" section describes the step in apply mode and goes live with that switch (`ai-transparency.md`, §3).

## Reader format

- **The projection** (`services/researchedStory.ts`, pure): `buildResearchedStory(run)` makes it once, at apply, from a run's draft, sources and earlier stories: the story's paragraphs, each stored as **one** cited passage in `paragraphs[].sentences`, and the Why This Matters and Caveats bullets, each with one citation per *source* (fact ids collapse to their source, first-cited order; ids not in the pool dropped; a source's date fact `S2F0` counts as citing it) carrying that source's first supporting sentence for the card; and the sources something cites (the quote's included), the original article (`S0`) always first and our earlier stories last. Hosts are kept without `www.` for the favicon route; a URL that is not http(s) is not served, and the schema itself refuses one (`url` is http(s) or null), so a stored projection holding a `javascript:` or other address fails the read-time parse below instead of reaching a link. The run's calls, drops and facts never reach it. **The shape is kept from the per-sentence format:** a version applied before 2026-11-13 holds each paragraph's sentences, each cited, and both parse with the same schema, so the reader shows one citation group after each passage either way; `sentences` is a misnomer for new versions, kept because `research` is in the public OpenAPI contract and renaming it would break it (the owner's call, `ci.md`, "Public API contract"). Both OpenAPI descriptions say so. `researchedStoryText` joins a paragraph's passages, so the podcast reads both.
- **At read time** (`getPublishedStoryBySlug`, `services/publicStories.ts`): the stored projection is parsed with `researchedStorySchema`; one query by id finds which earlier stories are still published, `forReaders` drops the others with their citations (and as the quote's source) and gives the rest their current slug. A projection that fails its parse is served as `research: null` with a warning.
- **`researched`** is served true whenever `pre_research` is set, independently of the projection, so the label stays honest: the plain fields then hold researched text, and the page shows them under the researched label (`public-website.md`, "Researched stories").
- **Only the single story** carries `research` and `researched` (`GET /api/stories/{slug}`, `PublicStoryDetail` in OpenAPI; the design's deviation): lists, homepage, search, related, RSS and the widget don't. Its `aiGenerated.fields` adds `research` when the projection is served (`AI_GENERATED_STORY_DETAIL_FIELDS`, `withAiGeneratedDetailMarker`); `analysis.test.ts`'s registry check stays on the list fields.
- **Source favicons** (`services/sourceFavicons.ts`, table `source_favicons` keyed by host): a 32 px PNG per outlet host, found by the feed favicons' search (`findIcon` in `favicon.ts`, Tavily's icon address tried first, every fetch through the SSRF guard) in the background after an apply (`fetchSourceFaviconsInBackground`; the server then contacts Google's favicon API, Tavily's icon addresses and the outlets' sites, with no visitor data); a host where none was found gets a null PNG and is not looked up again for 30 days (`config.favicon.sourceNegativeCacheDays`); a stored PNG is never refetched. `GET /api/source-favicons/:host` serves it (a plain lowercase host name only, else 404 without a lookup; 1-day cache, ETag, mounted before the shared rate limiter, not in OpenAPI, as the feed favicon route is not). Readers' browsers never ask the outlets.

| `POST` | `/api/admin/research/stories/:storyId/revert` | "Revert to single-source": `204`; 409 without a researched version, on a trashed story, or with an unreadable snapshot. Works with research switched off |

An editor's run is a one-story `research` task in `taskRegistry`, so the client follows it with `launchPolledTask` and the story counts as processing while it runs (bulk actions skip it); the research step's runs are not tasks. Any verdict, `fallback` included, completes the task; an unexpected error fails it, and so does a run marked interrupted before it finished.

## Admin UI

A Research section at the end of `StoryEditForm` (panel and page): "Admin only: a run you start changes nothing on the story until you use its version", the month's spend against the cap, the month's fallbacks by category, the Research button (a `ReasonButton`, visible with its reason when a run can't start), and a picker of the story's runs, each naming who started it (`editor` or `pipeline`). The run view opens with a badge, Editor or Pipeline (`RunTrigger`), and shows a $0 run the research step skipped as "Skipped by the research step: …", not as a failure; a run that failed after its research ("Failed after its research: …") names its measured cost, one that failed before it its reservation. The story list marks a story the research step rejected with a "Research" badge giving the reason (`StoryTable`); there is no filter by rejection reason. Under the picker, "Use this version" for a run that can be applied (`canUseVersion`, `ResearchVersion.tsx`); on a researched story, "This story shows a researched version" with "Revert to single-source" above the Research button. Both are `ReasonButton`s, disabled with the reason on a status that takes no research write ("Use this version" also with the usage's `applyRefusal`: research switched off, or the owner's gate closed), and both ask in a `ConfirmDialog` first, saying whether readers see the change at once (published) or once the story is published; on success every story query is re-read (`invalidateStoryQueries`), so the form shows the new values and its lock. While the story is researched the form disables the swapped fields and says "Revert to single-source first". "In progress" comes from the tab's own polled task or any `running` run in the list, which is re-read every 5 s while one runs. `ResearchRunView` shows the verdict (a fallback with its category; `research-rating` as "Recorded only: the story keeps its own rating and is not rejected"), the draft with its own rating marked as recorded only and its emotion tag beside the story's single-source one ("Research's emotion: calm (single-source: frustrating)", the latter from the snapshot on a researched story; no line for runs stored before 2026-11-14), its citation chips after each paragraph and bullet (after each sentence for a draft stored before 2026-11-13; the source's outlet, linked to its http(s) URL; "Actually Relevant" for our earlier coverage, linked to `/stories/<slug>`), what the checks found, each problem with its cause ("copying, AI check", "fact, code check"; none for runs stored before), and collapsible Sources, Facts and Cost. The section renders nothing while its usage query loads and hides itself when the usage endpoint answers 404, so a new client works against an old backend. The client's research types in `shared/types` are hand-written mirrors of the server's views; the route test pins the JSON shape.

## Modifying

- **A new step:** add it to `STEPS` and `STEP_TIERS` (`researchPipeline.ts`), to `STEP_OUTPUT_TOKENS` (`researchCalls.ts`), to the eval's `SCHEMA_NAMES` and `STEP_ESTIMATES` (`scripts/eval/research/pipeline.ts`) and to `ResearchStep` in `shared/types`.
- **A new tier model:** give it a row in `config.research.modelPrices`, or every run is refused.
- **A new written field in the draft:** add it to `draftItems` (`researchChecks.ts`), so both copying checks and the judge read it (one that carries no citations is judged against the whole pool, `checkedItems`), and decide whether `dropFailing` may drop it (`UNDROPPABLE`); merge a repair's answer for it in `withRepairs` (`researchRepair.ts`), or a repair round can never fix it. A new kind of source text shown to the writer goes in `sourceLines` (`researchCopying.ts`) under an id the write prompt shows too, or the checks no longer see everything the writer copies from. The key quote stays out of `draftItems`: it joins the fact check's items last (`checkedQuote`), and any new check that reads items must skip it for copying (part `quote`), as `checkCopying` and `judgedProblems` do.
- **The eval runs the same modules** (`.context/model-eval.md`, "Research measurement"), so a change to the prompts, filter, checks or pipeline changes `eval:research` too.
